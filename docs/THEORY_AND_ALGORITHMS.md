# Theory and algorithms

This chapter explains the general mathematics implemented by SPHINX. It
follows the derivation in J. M. Molina's *Geometric Structure-Preserving
Schrodinger-Maxwell Solver* notes (revision 7, August 4, 2025). The same
framework is used in the PRE manuscript *Self-Consistent Dynamics of Electron
Radiation Reaction via Structure-Preserving Geometric Algorithms for Coupled
Schrodinger-Maxwell Systems*, where radiation reaction is one application of
the general solver. Every matrix formula below is also checked against the
active MATLAB implementation. The sources give the derivation; the code
correspondence identifies what SPHINX actually computes.

## 1. The physical system

SPHINX evolves a nonrelativistic matter field self-consistently coupled to its
electromagnetic field:

$$
i\hbar\partial_t\psi=\widehat{H}\psi,
\qquad
\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=\mu_0J^\nu,
$$

with minimal-coupling Hamiltonian

$$
\widehat{H}=\frac{1}{2m}\left(-i\hbar\nabla-q\mathbf A\right)^2+V.
$$

The solver uses temporal gauge, $\phi=0$. Thus

$$
\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A,
\qquad
\mathbf E=-\partial_t\mathbf A,
$$

while $V$ is a prescribed, time-independent external scalar potential rather
than the electromagnetic scalar potential. With the canonical electromagnetic
momentum

$$
\mathbf Y\equiv\epsilon_0\dot{\mathbf A}=-\epsilon_0\mathbf E,
$$

the total Hamiltonian splits naturally into matter and field energies:

$$
H=H_{\mathrm{qm}}+H_{\mathrm{em}},
$$

$$
H_{\mathrm{qm}}=\int d^3x\,\psi^*\widehat H\psi,
\qquad
H_{\mathrm{em}}=\frac12\int d^3x
\left(\frac{|\mathbf Y|^2}{\epsilon_0}
+\frac{|\nabla\times\mathbf A|^2}{\mu_0}\right).
$$

Writing

$$
\psi=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(\psi_R+i\psi_I)
$$

makes $(\psi_R,\psi_I)$ a real canonical pair. Together with
$(\mathbf A,\mathbf Y)$, the continuous Poisson bracket is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\{F,G\}={}&\frac1\hbar\int d^3x
\left(
\frac{\delta F}{\delta\psi_R}\frac{\delta G}{\delta\psi_I}
-\frac{\delta G}{\delta\psi_R}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\psi_I}
\right)\\
&+\int d^3x
\left(
\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mathbf A}\cdot\frac{\delta G}{\delta\mathbf Y}
-\frac{\delta G}{\delta\mathbf A}\cdot\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mathbf Y}
\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

Hamilton's equations generated by this bracket are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot\psi_R={}&\frac{q}{2m}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf A)\psi_R
+\frac qm\mathbf A\cdot\nabla\psi_R
+\frac{1}{2m\hbar}\left(-\hbar^2\nabla^2+q^2|\mathbf A|^2+2mV\right)\psi_I,\\
\dot\psi_I={}&\frac{q}{2m}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf A)\psi_I
+\frac qm\mathbf A\cdot\nabla\psi_I
+\frac{1}{2m\hbar}\left(\hbar^2\nabla^2-q^2|\mathbf A|^2-2mV\right)\psi_R,\\
\dot{\mathbf A}={}&\frac{\mathbf Y}{\epsilon_0},\\
\dot{\mathbf Y}={}&\mathbf J-\frac1{\mu_0}\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf A,
\end{aligned}
$$

where the self-consistent current is

$$
\mathbf J=\frac{q}{2m}
\left[
\hbar(\psi_R\nabla\psi_I-\psi_I\nabla\psi_R)
-q(\psi_R^2+\psi_I^2)\mathbf A
\right].
$$

The first part is the phase-gradient, or paramagnetic, current. The second is
the vector-potential, or diamagnetic, current. This $\mathbf J$ is the
nonlinear feedback channel: the wavefunction drives $\mathbf Y$, which changes
$\mathbf A$, which changes the quantum Hamiltonian.

## 2. Why the real-imaginary formulation matters

For a finite spatial discretization with $M$ scalar degrees of freedom, define

$$
S=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_R\\\psi_I\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb R^{2M}.
$$

The quantum dynamics have the form

$$
\dot S=\Gamma(\mathbf A)S,
$$

where the correctly assembled $\Gamma$ is both skew-symmetric and
infinitesimally symplectic:

$$
\Gamma^T=-\Gamma,
\qquad
\Gamma^T\mathbb J+\mathbb J\Gamma=0,
\qquad
\mathbb J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\-I&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\Gamma\in\mathfrak{so}(2M)\cap\mathfrak{sp}(2M,\mathbb R)
\cong\mathfrak u(M).
$$

This identity is the geometric reason that one can preserve both the quantum
norm and the canonical symplectic form. In finite dimensions,

$$
SO(2M)\cap Sp(2M,\mathbb R)\cong U(M).
$$

SPHINX explicitly checks `O' == -O` before each quantum solve. A failure is not
treated as a small numerical defect: it means the discrete operator no longer
belongs to the required Lie algebra.

## 3. Spatial discretization

Let $J=(i,j,k)$ index an Eulerian grid cell of volume
$\Delta V=\Delta x\Delta y\Delta z$. Piecewise-constant deposition gives, for
example,

$$
\psi_R(t,\mathbf x)=\sum_{J=1}^M\psi_{R,J}(t)
\theta(\mathbf x-\mathbf x_J),
$$

with analogous expansions for $\psi_I$, $\mathbf A$, and $\mathbf Y$. The
discrete canonical bracket becomes

$$
\begin{aligned}
\{F,G\}_d=\sum_{J=1}^M\frac1{\Delta V}\Bigg[&
\frac1\hbar\left(
\frac{\partial F}{\partial\psi_{R,J}}
\frac{\partial G}{\partial\psi_{I,J}}
-\frac{\partial G}{\partial\psi_{R,J}}
\frac{\partial F}{\partial\psi_{I,J}}
\right)\\
&+\frac{\partial F}{\partial\mathbf A_J}\cdot
\frac{\partial G}{\partial\mathbf Y_J}
-\frac{\partial G}{\partial\mathbf A_J}\cdot
\frac{\partial F}{\partial\mathbf Y_J}
\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
$$

### Backward-difference operators

For a scalar field $u$,

$$
(D_xu)_{i,j,k}=\frac{u_{i,j,k}-u_{i-1,j,k}}{\Delta x},
$$

and similarly for $D_y$ and $D_z$. SPHINX stacks these operators into

$$
G=\begin{pmatrix}D_x\\D_y\\D_z\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb R^{3M\times M}.
$$

The implemented backward second difference is

$$
(L_xu)_{i,j,k}
=\frac{u_{i,j,k}-2u_{i-1,j,k}+u_{i-2,j,k}}{\Delta x^2},
$$

with $L=L_x+L_y+L_z$. It is deliberately paired with its transpose in the
quantum generator; the symmetric kinetic operator is $(L+L^T)$, not $2L$ by
assumption.

The discrete curl is assembled from the same component differences:

$$
C=\begin{pmatrix}
0&-D_z&D_y\\
D_z&0&-D_x\\
-D_y&D_x&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Consequently, the magnetic-energy variation is represented by

$$
C^TC\mathbf A,
$$

the discrete counterpart of $\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf A$. This adjoint
pairing is essential: replacing it casually by a separately coded second curl
would generally break the variational origin of the map.

Periodic operators are implemented in one, two, and three dimensions. The
historical fixed-boundary curl and curl-curl operators are retained only for a
square two-dimensional grid.

## 4. Flattened state and exact storage order

Let $M=N_xN_yN_z$. SPHINX stores

$$
S=[\psi_R,\psi_I]\in\mathbb R^{2M},
$$

and

$$
F=[A_x,A_y,A_z,Y_x,Y_y,Y_z]\in\mathbb R^{6M}.
$$

Within each scalar block, $x$ varies fastest, followed by $y$, then $z$:

$$
\ell(i,j,k)=i+(j-1)N_x+(k-1)N_xN_y.
$$

The arrays entering MATLAB are shaped `[y,x,z]`; each $x$-$y$ plane is
transposed before flattening. This convention explains the transpose in
`flattenFields` and must be preserved by post-production code.

Only two rows of each state are held in memory. Row one contains the beginning
of a global step, row two is the work/output row, and the completed row is
rolled back into row one. Requested time slices are written to disk.

## 5. Nondimensionalization and the coefficients in the code

Use reference scales

$$
\mathbf x=\lambda\widetilde{\mathbf x},\quad
t=\tau\widetilde t,\quad
\psi=\psi_0\widetilde\psi,\quad
\mathbf A=A_0\widetilde{\mathbf A},\quad
\mathbf Y=Y_0\widetilde{\mathbf Y},\quad
V=E_0\widetilde V.
$$

`importParams_sim` defines

$$
\lambda=\frac{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2}{|e|m_e},
\qquad
E_0=\frac{\hbar^2}{m_e\lambda^2},
\qquad
\tau=\frac\hbar{E_0},
\qquad
\psi_0=\lambda^{-3/2},
$$

$$
B_0=\frac\hbar{|e|\lambda^2},
\qquad
A_0=B_0\lambda,
\qquad
Y_0=\epsilon_0\frac{E_0}{|e|\lambda}.
$$

The map coefficients retained explicitly by the implementation are

$$
C_Y=\frac{\tau Y_0}{A_0\epsilon_0},
\qquad
C_A=\frac{\tau A_0c^2\epsilon_0}{\lambda^2Y_0},
$$

$$
C_1=\frac{\tau qA_0}{2m\lambda},
\quad
C_2=\frac{\hbar\tau}{4m\lambda^2},
\quad
C_3=\frac{\tau q^2A_0^2}{2m\hbar},
\quad
C_4=\frac{\tau E_0}{\hbar},
$$

and

$$
C_{JQ}=\frac{q\hbar\psi_0^2\tau}{2m\lambda Y_0},
\qquad
C_{JA}=\frac{q^2A_0\psi_0^2\tau}{2mY_0}.
$$

Keeping these constants named makes unit changes, species changes, and general
parameter studies auditable. The bundled defaults are atomic units:
$q=m=\hbar=1$, $c=137$, and $\epsilon_0=(4\pi)^{-1}$.

## 6. Electromagnetic submap

Under $H_{d,\mathrm{em}}$, $S$ is frozen and the flattened electromagnetic state
obeys

$$
\frac{dF}{d\widetilde t}=QF,
$$

with

$$
Q=\begin{pmatrix}
0&C_YI_{3M}\\
-C_AC^TC&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For fixed two-dimensional boundaries, the lower-left block uses the preserved
fixed-boundary curl-curl matrix in place of $C^TC$.

Implicit midpoint gives

$$
\left(I-\frac{\Delta\widetilde t}{2}Q\right)F^{*}
=\left(I+\frac{\Delta\widetilde t}{2}Q\right)F^n,
$$

or equivalently

$$
F^{*}=\mathrm{Cay}\left(\frac{\Delta\widetilde t}{2}Q\right)F^n,
\qquad
\mathrm{Cay}(X)=(I-X)^{-1}(I+X).
$$

`M_em_v2` does not form the inverse. It solves the sparse linear system with
BiCGSTAB and an ILU preconditioner. Because $Q$ is constant for a fixed grid,
boundary choice, and timestep, SPHINX constructs the EM ILU factors once and
reuses them for every global step.

## 7. Quantum submap

The quantum map holds the newly advanced $\mathbf A^*$ fixed while evolving
$S$. Let

$$
\mathcal A=\mathrm{diag}(A_x)D_x
+\mathrm{diag}(A_y)D_y
+\mathrm{diag}(A_z)D_z,
$$

and define the adjoint-weighted term

$$
\mathcal H_A
=D_x^T\mathrm{diag}(A_x)
+D_y^T\mathrm{diag}(A_y)
+D_z^T\mathrm{diag}(A_z).
$$

In MATLAB, elementwise multiplication constructs these sparse row-scaled
matrices in `M_qm_quantumFields_v3` and `makeHmatrix_v4`. Also define

$$
A^2=\mathrm{diag}(A_x^2+A_y^2+A_z^2),
\qquad
V_d=\mathrm{diag}(V).
$$

The two $M\times M$ blocks are

$$
\Omega_{\parallel}=C_1(\mathcal A-\mathcal H_A),
$$

$$
\Omega_{\perp}=C_2(L+L^T)-(C_3A^2+C_4V_d),
$$

and the actual code generator is

$$
\Omega(\mathbf A^*)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\Omega_{\parallel}&-\Omega_{\perp}\\
\Omega_{\perp}&\Omega_{\parallel}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The adjoint pairs make $\Omega_{\parallel}$ skew-symmetric and
$\Omega_{\perp}$ symmetric, hence

$$
\Omega^T=-\Omega.
$$

The quantum update is another implicit-midpoint/Cayley solve:

$$
\left(I-\frac{\Delta\widetilde t}{2}\Omega\right)S^{n+1}
=\left(I+\frac{\Delta\widetilde t}{2}\Omega\right)S^n.
$$

Because the Cayley transform of a real skew-symmetric matrix is orthogonal,

$$
\|S^{n+1}\|_2=\|S^n\|_2
$$

up to the iterative-solve tolerance. Because $\Omega$ is also infinitesimally
symplectic, the same map preserves the canonical symplectic structure. The
wavefunction step is therefore the real representation of a unitary map.

Unlike $Q$, $\Omega(\mathbf A^*)$ changes with the vector potential. Its ILU
preconditioner is rebuilt every timestep.

## 8. Midpoint current feedback

The quantum submap leaves $\mathbf A$ fixed but feeds the quantum current back
into $\mathbf Y$. SPHINX evaluates

$$
\psi_R^{n+1/2}=\frac{\psi_R^n+\psi_R^{n+1}}2,
\qquad
\psi_I^{n+1/2}=\frac{\psi_I^n+\psi_I^{n+1}}2,
$$

then computes, component by component,

$$
\widetilde{\mathbf J}
=C_{JQ}\left(
\psi_R^{n+1/2}G\psi_I^{n+1/2}
-\psi_I^{n+1/2}G\psi_R^{n+1/2}
\right)
-C_{JA}\left[
(\psi_R^{n+1/2})^2+(\psi_I^{n+1/2})^2
\right]\mathbf A^*.
$$

All products between equally sized field vectors in this expression are
pointwise. The final kick is

$$
\mathbf A^{n+1}=\mathbf A^*,
\qquad
\mathbf Y^{n+1}=\mathbf Y^*+\Delta\widetilde t\,\widetilde{\mathbf J}.
$$

This midpoint evaluation is not a post-processing diagnostic. It is part of
the Hamiltonian quantum subflow and is required for the stated geometric map.

## 9. One complete SPHINX timestep

The active solver uses the first-order Lie composition

$$
\eta(\Delta t)
=\eta_{\mathrm{qm}}(\Delta t)\circ\eta_{\mathrm{em}}(\Delta t).
$$

Applied right to left, one step is:

1. Hold $S^n$ fixed and solve the EM Cayley system for
   $(\mathbf A^*,\mathbf Y^*)$.
2. For the fixed two-dimensional path, restore the prescribed boundary entries
   of $F^*$.
3. Build $\Omega(\mathbf A^*)$ and solve the quantum Cayley system for
   $S^{n+1}$.
4. Form $S^{n+1/2}$, compute $\mathbf J^{n+1/2}$, and kick
   $\mathbf Y^*\mapsto\mathbf Y^{n+1}$ while holding $\mathbf A^*$ fixed.
5. Save the requested global timestep and roll the two-row state buffers.

In compact form,

$$
(S^n,F^n)
\longrightarrow
(S^n,F^*)
\longrightarrow
(S^{n+1},F^*)
\longrightarrow
(S^{n+1},F^{n+1}).
$$

The three arrows denote, in order, the electromagnetic Cayley map, the quantum
Cayley map, and the midpoint-current kick.

The manuscript also derives the symmetric second-order composition

$$
\eta^{[2]}(\Delta t)=
\eta_{\mathrm{qm}}(\Delta t/2)\circ
\eta_{\mathrm{em}}(\Delta t)\circ
\eta_{\mathrm{qm}}(\Delta t/2),
$$

and the recursive higher-order construction

$$
\eta^{[2n+2]}(\Delta t)=
\eta^{[2n]}(\alpha\Delta t)\circ
\eta^{[2n]}(\beta\Delta t)\circ
\eta^{[2n]}(\alpha\Delta t),
$$

$$
\alpha=\frac1{2-2^{1/(2n+1)}},
\qquad
\beta=1-2\alpha.
$$

These are mathematically available extensions, but they are **not** the
stepping sequence currently executed by `schrodingerMaxwellSolver_v16`.

## 10. What is preserved, and what that claim means

### Symplecticity

Each exact Cayley submap lies in the Lie group associated with its generator;
compositions of symplectic maps are symplectic. Numerically, the sparse linear
systems are solved only to finite tolerance, so preservation is realized to the
accuracy of the iterative solve.

### Unitarity and particle number

The quantum Cayley map is orthogonal in $(\psi_R,\psi_I)$ and therefore unitary
in complex form. With a uniform cell volume,

$$
N=\int|\psi|^2d^3x
\approx\frac{\Delta V}{2}\left(\|\psi_R\|_2^2+\|\psi_I\|_2^2\right)
$$

is invariant under the converged quantum solve. The factor $1/2$ follows from
$\psi=(\psi_R+i\psi_I)/\sqrt2$; diagnostics must use the convention of the
initialized fields consistently.

### Gauge-compatible spatial structure

The discrete gradient, curl, their transposes, and the current are derived from
one discrete Hamiltonian rather than selected independently. This compatible
construction is the basis of the gauge-invariance claim. Boundary replacement
on the historical fixed path is an additional implementation operation and
should be assessed separately from the periodic variational construction.

### Energy behavior

Symplecticity does not mean that the first-order split map conserves the exact
continuous Hamiltonian at every step. It normally produces bounded,
structure-controlled energy error and exactly follows a nearby modified
Hamiltonian. Energy partition diagnostics remain essential in any coupled
matter-field study.

## 11. Linear solves and validity

For both Cayley maps, SPHINX solves

$$
M_-x=M_+b,
\qquad
M_\pm=I\pm\frac{\Delta\widetilde t}{2}K,
$$

using ILU-preconditioned BiCGSTAB with relative tolerance $10^{-8}$ and at most
1000 iterations. It records the flag, achieved relative residual, and iteration
count for every EM and QM substep.

A run is `converged` only when every active flag is zero. A run marked
`completed_with_solver_warnings` may contain complete-looking output files, but
those files are not evidence that the geometric maps were solved accurately.

## 13. Model switches

The public `model` option changes which pieces of the same map are active:

| Model | EM Cayley | Quantum Cayley | Midpoint current kick |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `EM` | yes | frozen | no |
| `QM` | frozen | yes | no |
| `both` | yes | yes | yes |

This makes the uncoupled modes useful controls. `QM` studies unitary evolution
in the prescribed vector potential; `EM` studies source-free field propagation;
`both` activates the nonlinear, self-consistent matter-field feedback channel.

## 14. Scientific qualifications

- The active time composition is first order even though each individual
  Cayley subsolve is an implicit-midpoint step.
- The fixed-boundary implementation is a preserved historical two-dimensional
  path; periodic boundaries are the general one-, two-, and three-dimensional
  path.
- The current implementation fixes $\psi_0=\lambda^{-3/2}$ even for reduced
  dimensional runs. This is a three-dimensional physical normalization applied
  to fields represented on lower-dimensional computational grids.
- The bundled cyclotron example is extruded uniformly through active $z$
  slices in 3D. Its normalization and physical interpretation should be
  independently checked before arbitrary 3D production use. This qualification
  concerns that initializer, not the general numerical maps.
- The temporal-gauge formulation does not eliminate constraint checks. Charge
  continuity, Gauss-law behavior, norm, energy partition, and solver residuals
  should all be monitored in serious runs.

## 15. Reading the algorithm physically

The EM Cayley step first propagates the field already present on the lattice.
The resulting vector potential defines the kinetic momentum seen by the matter
field. The quantum Cayley step then rotates the wavefunction in a way that
preserves its norm and canonical geometry. Finally, the midpoint probability
flow supplies the current that changes the electric canonical momentum. The
next global step begins with that altered electromagnetic state.

The algorithm therefore describes self-consistent exchange rather than adding
a problem-specific phenomenological term: matter shapes the electromagnetic
field through $\mathbf J$, and the field reshapes matter through minimal
coupling. Radiation reaction is one example of physics that can emerge from
this closed evolution; it is not hard-coded into SPHINX.
